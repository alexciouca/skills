# Bolder

Give a safe, forgettable design a point of view. Make one or two big moves,
not ten small ones; boldness comes from commitment, not from adding things.

**Keeps:** content, structure of the main task, brand elements the project
pins, the quality floor.
**May change:** scale, weight, color strength, composition, imagery, motion,
within what the mode allows.

## Steps

1. **Find the idea.** What is the one thing this screen should make people
   feel or remember? Write it in one line. No idea, no bolder: ask.
2. **Look at the neighbors.** A flat section often skips the moves the rest
   of the page already makes: the display type at full size, the signature
   detail, the density. Bring it up to that level in the page's own
   vocabulary before inventing new moves.
3. **Pick levers, strongest first.** Usually one or two are enough:
   - **Scale contrast:** make the most important thing much bigger, and the
     rest smaller. Timid designs keep everything medium.
   - **Weight and type contrast:** a heavy or characterful headline against
     a quiet body.
   - **One strong color:** a committed accent used on few things, or a bold
     background for one section. Not more colors.
   - **Composition:** asymmetry, an element that crops off the edge, an
     unexpected order, generous empty space next to something dense.
   - **Real imagery:** a large, specific photo or illustration instead of
     generic icons.
   - **Motion:** last, and only for one moment that supports the idea.
4. **Remove what competes.** Bolder often means fewer elements, so the bold
   one has room.
5. **Gray-box test.** Replace the copy with gray bars in your head (or in a
   screenshot). If the structure alone no longer shows what matters, the
   boldness lives in the words, not the design. Fix the structure.
6. **Check the floor.** Contrast, focus, targets, reading comfort still pass.
   Bold is not an excuse for unreadable.

## How far, by mode

- **Sell / Show:** go far. A clear, even surprising, visual idea is the job.
- **Read:** bolder headings, a striking opening, strong pull quotes or
  figures; the body text stays calm.
- **Work:** bolder means clearer, not louder: stronger hierarchy, one accent
  for the primary action and status, sharper type contrast. Never at the cost
  of scanning or consistency.

## Don't

Add a gradient, glow or glass effect and call it bold; make everything bigger
at once; add a second and third accent color; animate everything.

Report the idea, the levers you used, and before/after screenshots.
