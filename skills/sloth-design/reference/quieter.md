# Quieter

Calm a loud, crowded or tiring design so the important things can be heard.
Quieter is not grayer: keep the character, lose the noise.

**Keeps:** content, the main task, the one or two elements that make the
product recognisable (a color, a typeface, a shape). Name them before you
start.
**May change:** number of colors, weights, sizes, effects, borders,
animations, decorative elements, density.

## Steps

1. **Count the noise.** List how many colors, font sizes, weights, shadows,
   borders, badges, animations and competing calls to action are on the
   screen. Most loud designs are loud from quantity, not from one thing.
2. **Remove before restyling.** Delete decoration that carries no meaning:
   duplicate dividers, background shapes, icons next to every label,
   badges nobody reads.
3. **Reduce:**
   - colors to one accent plus neutrals; status colors only for status;
   - sizes and weights to the type scale (see `typeset.md` rules);
   - shadows to one level for raised things; borders or shadows, not both;
   - animation to feedback only, shorter and smaller.
4. **One focal point.** Pick the most important element; lower the volume of
   everything around it rather than raising it further.
5. **Give space.** More room between groups, fewer things per row, lighter
   containers.
6. **Soften, carefully.** Lower saturation of large surfaces, keep strong
   color for small important things. Keep contrast above the floor; quiet
   text must still be readable.

## How far, by mode

- **Work:** quiet is the default target; status and the primary action keep
  their color.
- **Read:** the text column should feel calm; strip chrome around it.
- **Sell / Show:** keep the one big idea; quiet everything that competes with
  it.

## Don't

Turn everything light gray; remove the brand's one distinctive element;
lower contrast below the floor; hide actions users need.

Report what you removed, what you reduced (with counts before and after), and
before/after screenshots.
